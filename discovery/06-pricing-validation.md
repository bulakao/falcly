# Falcly — Pricing Validation

> **Date:** 2026-03-30
> **For:** Falcly discovery

---

## ✅ Final Pricing Decision

**Model:** Free + Pro only. No tiers, no complexity. Validate willingness-to-pay before adding tiers.

| Plan | US | PH | Cards |
|------|----|----|-------|
| Free | $0 | ₱0 | 2 cards |
| Pro | $4.99/mo or $39.99/yr | ₱149/mo or ₱999/yr | Unlimited |

**Rationale:**
- $4.99/mo US: below YNAB/Monarch ceiling ($9.99–$14.99), "no-brainer" price point, no competitor in this space to anchor against
- ₱149/mo PH: anchors to Netflix pricing, clears "worth it" threshold without sticker shock
- Annual plans (US $39.99, PH ₱999) are the primary CTA — ~$3.33/mo and ~₱83/mo respectively
- Dropped original 4-tier model (Free/$9.99/$19.99/Enterprise) — premature, positions against YNAB incorrectly

**Risk to monitor:** $4.99 may undervalue the product if no competitor exists. Revisit at 1,000 Pro users.

---

## Executive Summary

Falcly's proposed tiered pricing (Free / $9.99 / $19.99) is well-positioned within the personal finance app market. The $9.99/mo Standard tier sits below YNAB ($14.99), Monarch ($14.99), and Copilot ($13), while above Simplifi ($5.99). For the Philippines, ₱149–₱199/mo is the right range — below the PH user pain threshold while above commodity pricing.

The key pricing insight: Falcly is cheaper than one late fee. A single credit card late fee in the US is $25–$41. In the Philippines, it's ₱500–₱1,500. The annual subscription pays for itself if it prevents one late payment.

## 4. Philippine Market Pricing

### Why ₱149–₱199/mo

- GCash/Maya premium features are mostly free (subsidized by volume)
- PH SaaS app ceiling: ₱99–₱299/mo for most users
- NCR professionals (primary target): tolerate ₱199–₱499/mo for genuine value
- Median monthly income: ~₱20,583–₱41,300

### Revenue Modeling (PH)

| Scenario | Paid Users | ARPU | Monthly Revenue | Annual Revenue |
|----------|-----------|------|-----------------|----------------|
| Conservative | 10K | ₱149/mo | ₱1.49M (~$26K) | ₱17.9M (~$311K) |
| Moderate | 50K | ₱179/mo | ₱8.95M (~$155K) | ₱107.4M (~$1.86M) |
| Aggressive | 100K | ₱199/mo | ₱19.9M (~$346K) | ₱238.8M (~$4.15M) |

At 3–5% conversion of active users, 100K paid users requires ~2–3.3M active free users — achievable given PH's 13.9M credit cards and 85% Android penetration.

## 5. Willingness to Pay Analysis

### What users say (Reddit, forums):

- **YNAB users:** "Worth every penny for budgeting but I need something for my credit card cycles too"
- **r/PHCreditCards:** Users manually track cutoff dates in Notes/spreadsheets — zero cost but high friction
- **r/CreditCards:** "I'd pay $5/mo for something that just tells me where I am in each card's cycle"
- **Post-Mint users:** Paying $10–$15/mo for Monarch/YNAB but still lack billing cycle features

### Key Insight: Two Segments

| Segment | Price Sensitivity | What They Buy |
|---------|-------------------|---------------|
| **Anxiety-driven** (avoid late fees) | Low — it's insurance | Peace of mind |
| **Optimization-driven** (utilization management) | Moderate | Credit score improvement |

Falcly serves both but should market to the anxiety-driven segment first — lower price sensitivity, higher urgency.

---

## 6. Pricing Recommendations

1. **Keep Free tier meaningful** — 2 cards with full safe-to-swipe logic. This is the hook. Don't cripple it.
2. **$9.99/mo is validated** — below premium budgeting apps, above utility apps. Right tier for "credit card intelligence."
3. **PH pricing at ₱149–₱199/mo** — accessible but not throwaway. Consider ₱149 at launch, increase to ₱199 after traction.
4. **Annual discount: 20–25%** — $7.99/mo equivalent ($95.88/yr) for annual. PH: ₱1,499/yr (~₱125/mo).
5. **No Enterprise tier at launch** — the workspace/household feature can be Premium-gated, not Enterprise-gated.
